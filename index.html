Featured Case Study

Orbit — Listen Together

A mobile music-streaming concept exploring how shared listening can feel social, immediate and simple — even when friends are apart.

The challenge

Music streaming is usually a solo experience.

Orbit explores a way for people to listen to music and podcasts together in real time without relying on manual link-sharing or complicated setup. The goal was to make shared listening feel like a natural part of the product, not an extra feature.

Product DesignEnd-to-End UI/UXMobileFigma

The solution

Users can create or join an Orbit — a shared listening room where friends stay on the same playback position while keeping useful personal controls.

  • Start or join a live listening session
  • Invite friends through a simple QR flow
  • Keep the group synchronized in real time
  • Discover content through personalized onboarding

Design focus

I focused on balancing the social layer with a familiar music-player experience. Shared actions remain clear, while personal audio control stays available to each listener.

  • Clear host and guest roles
  • Low-friction joining and inviting
  • Familiar playback patterns
  • A visual language built around the Orbit metaphor

Outcome

One system. One Orbit.

The final concept brings onboarding, discovery, playback and shared listening into one visual system. This case study is an exploration of the product idea and interface direction; the shared-listening hypothesis would be the next thing to validate through usability testing and behavioral data.

DogGo — Dog Walking, Made Simple

An earlier UX exploration of a two-sided mobile service connecting dog owners with walkers.

Earlier UX exploration

Designing for both sides of one service

DogGo was an earlier UX exploration focused on designing a two-sided service for dog owners and walkers. The project helped me understand how different user needs translate into connected flows within the same product.

UX ExplorationTwo-Sided PlatformProduct DesignMobile
From discovery to booking

Mapping the owner journey before designing the interface.

I broke the core owner journey into two focused flows: discovering the right walker and completing the booking with as little friction as possible.

01 · Search & Discovery Flow

I mapped the discovery flow to keep the path from search to walker selection simple, while also considering the no-results scenario.

02 · Main Action Flow

Once a walker is selected, the booking flow is intentionally short, reducing the number of steps between decision and confirmation.

Selected interface screens

Two perspectives, one connected service.

These selected screens show the product from both sides: the owner can discover and follow an active walk, while the walker can manage requests and track the walk in real time.

Owner & Walker · Selected screens
What I learned

Two users. One shared experience.

Designing DogGo taught me to think beyond individual screens and focus on how two user journeys connect within one product. Working through both sides helped me understand the importance of clear information architecture, consistent flows and designing around different user needs within the same system.
